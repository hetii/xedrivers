# aic8800: AICSemi AIC8800D80

Directory: `src/drivers/aic8800/` · `a69c:8d80`, `a69c:8d81` · USB Wi-Fi 6 and
Bluetooth

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `aic_fw.c` | firmware load into the boot ROM |
| `aic_wifi.c` | the Wi-Fi firmware's bring-up, scan and join |
| `aic_data.c` | frames, reordering hooks, the temperature cap |
| `aic_cmd.c` | messages to the firmware |
| `aic_bt.c` | Bluetooth (HCI) |

The card is offered to `src/wireless/` as a `WL_CARD` ([xedrivers.md](../xedrivers.md#wi-fi)).

## How it works

### Bring-up

1. The chip arrives as `a69c:8d80`, its boot ROM.
2. The driver writes the firmware into it from the `aic8800D80` directory beside
   the plugin, and starts it.
3. The chip comes back as `a69c:8d81`, with the Wi-Fi firmware running.

Both phases send exactly the messages aicsemi's Linux driver sends. They are
checked against its own usbmon recording in `tests/drivers/aic8800/`. Revision U01
chips need other files and are only recognised.

The station uses the console's own MAC address, read from its configuration
(category 2, setting 1) before any device is brought up. xnet sends from that
address and takes in nothing else.

### Scanning and joining

- Scans fill a table of networks (`tools/xectl.py status`).
- `xectl.py cmd 31 <index>` joins one; `cmd 32` leaves and stays off until
  `cmd 31`.
- Joining by itself: `wifi_ssid` in `xedrivers.ini` (README, Configuration).

### WPA2

The card's firmware does the ciphering, given the keys. The plugin runs only the
four-way handshake (`src/wireless/wpa.c`).

- Every hash and block cipher is the console kernel's own (`XeCryptHmacSha*`,
  `XeCryptSha*`, `XeCryptAes*`).
- The association asks the firmware to keep the controlled port shut and pass EAPOL
  through.
- Keys are installed from the worker: the firmware confirms each one, and that
  cannot be waited for where frames arrive.
- The link is reported up only then, so xnet runs DHCP after the port opens.
- `xectl.py cmd 20` checks the two awkward parts against published examples: the
  key from a passphrase (IEEE 802.11i) and the group key unwrap (RFC 3394).

### Receiving

- **Buffers of 20 KB.** The firmware aggregates received frames into one bulk
  transfer of up to 20480 bytes (`AICWF_USB_AGGR_MAX_PKT_SIZE`). The ring is eight
  buffers of 20 KB. With 4 KB buffers a transfer was cut and the rest began the
  next buffer mid-packet.
- **Reordering.** The firmware hands up an A-MPDU's frames as they decode, so a
  retried frame arrives after the ones behind it (3.7% of frames under load). xnet
  answers each with a duplicate ACK and the sender halves its window.
  `src/wireless/reorder.c` puts them back in order: a window of 64 per TID, a hole
  given up after two worker ticks. One stream in went from 60 Mbit/s (7.5 MB/s) to
  104 Mbit/s (13.0 MB/s).

### The temperature cap

Every second the driver reads the chip's temperature from the firmware
(TEMP_COMP_GET, `MM_SET_VENDOR_SWCONFIG_REQ`).

| Reading | What the driver does |
|---|---|
| 116 or more | caps sending, first at 100 Mbit/s, then 25% lower each second, down to 12 Mbit/s |
| between 110 and 116 | keeps the cap |
| below 110 | raises the cap 12.5% a second; lifts it at 400 Mbit/s |
| no answer | keeps the cap as it is and retries after 10 s; a firmware that never answered 3 tries is left uncapped |

Why it is needed: see Issues. Thresholds 120/115 let the chip reach 126 and gave no
more throughput.

### Bluetooth

The dongle is one composite device. Its first interface is standard HCI over USB
and needs no vendor step once the firmware is in.

- The Wi-Fi driver drives it through the same device node (`aic_bt.c`).
- Commands are class control requests; events come on interrupt endpoint 0x83.
- `USB_DEV` keeps eight endpoint slots, which Wi-Fi and Bluetooth together need.

So far it is reset and asked for its version and address: HCI version 13 revision
11, address one past the Wi-Fi side's (`xectl.py status`, the `bt` line).

## Issues

- **The card overheats when it sends.** Without the cap it drops off the router
  after minutes of sustained sending ("disconnected due to excessive missing
  ACKs").
  - The temperature reading goes from 92 to 127, as far as it reads, in about 25 s
    of sending flat out. The radio then stops both ways for about 40 s.
  - Receiving at 119 Mbit/s it settles at 108.
  - Rate control held VHT-MCS 9 at 94 to 99% success up to the stop.
  - Neither the firmware's temperature compensation nor 6 dBm instead of 14 to 18
    slowed the rise.
  - Both dongles on Linux with aicsemi's driver do the same: 60 to 115 Mbit/s for
    30 s, then 0 to 1 Mbit/s.
  - The RTL8822BU on the same router ran the same load for 20 min without a
    disconnect.

  The card itself does not hold a sustained upload. The cap keeps the link at the
  cost of throughput. (`xerunner: aic8800-throughput-findings@2`)
- **One transmit transfer at a time.** The chip takes one transfer at a time, 0.16
  to 0.3 ms each, so every ACK the console sends costs a transfer. Tried and
  dropped:
  - several frames in one transfer: the firmware stops transmitting (its TX_AGGR
    patch settings exist only for the 8800DC);
  - merging queued pure ACKs behind a cap on transfers in flight: +13% at eight
    streams, the link lost with the cap at two.
- **The same card as a PC's access point is a poor test partner.**
  - aicsemi's Linux driver is built with `CONFIG_FILTER_TCP_ACK=y`: it holds TCP
    ACKs up to 5 ms and drops superseded ones. The console, with few segments in
    flight, waits 5 ms every round: one stream out 8 Mbit/s (1.0 MB/s) instead of
    101 Mbit/s (12.6 MB/s).
  - Its firmware degrades under long load and hung three times.

  Measure against a real router.
- **Placement and channel matter more than the driver.** Through a floor, -73 dBm on
  a busy channel gave 4 Mbit/s (0.5 MB/s) out; -62 dBm on a free channel gave
  48 Mbit/s (6.0 MB/s). Through a floor 2.4 GHz may beat 5 GHz, so `wifi_ssid`
  takes both names.
- **Bluetooth is only brought up.** No connection, pairing or audio (that would need
  L2CAP and a codec).

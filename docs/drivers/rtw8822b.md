# rtw8822b: Realtek RTL8822BU

Directory: `src/drivers/rtw8822b/` · `0bda:b812` · USB Wi-Fi 5, 2x2

A port of Linux rtw88's RTL8822B support. The register tables
(`rtw8822b_table.c`) are imported from rtw88 with `tools/rtw88_import.py`.

| Part | Where |
|---|---|
| power on, firmware, efuse, MAC, baseband, radio | this directory |
| scanning, joining, block ack, data path | the SoftMAC station, `src/wireless/softmac/` |
| which network to join and when | the Wi-Fi mode ([xedrivers.md](../xedrivers.md#wi-fi)) |

## How it works

Bring-up follows rtw88's probe, power_on and core_start:

1. the MAC is powered;
2. the firmware is downloaded and started;
3. the efuse is read for the card's address;
4. the MAC, baseband and radio are set up;
5. a station port is set on that address and receive starts.

The firmware is `firmware\rtw88\rtw8822b_fw.bin` beside the plugin, the same path
as in linux-firmware: v30.20.0 (linux-firmware a50c068b). Against v27.2.0 it is
the same within 2% each way, TCP and ftpdll (2026-11-02). It stays in memory for a
later bring-up.

What sets the throughput:

| Setting | Why |
|---|---|
| transmit power from the efuse and the by-rate tables, no regulatory limits; the firmware runs IQK | the AP hears the console at VHT-MCS 9 2SS |
| firmware rate adaptation, block ack both ways, receive aggregation | full rates both ways; the aggregation timeout (0x10) sets how often the host controller stops asking for receive transfers |
| SM power save static off in the HT capability | the AP sends on two streams, not one |
| up to 16 frames in one USB transfer, from the first frame out | one stream out 157 to 169 Mbit/s (19.6 to 21.1 MB/s) |
| a completion sends the open aggregate only below 4 transfers out, unless it holds a short frame (`EP_KEEP_OUT`, `SMALL_FRAME`) | transfers leave full (16 frames, 915 a second, not 1.8 frames 7540 a second): 4 and 8 streams out 156 to 170 Mbit/s (19.5 to 21.2 MB/s); ACKs still go at once |
| at most 16 transmit transfers in flight | same speed as 32, and the host controller stops asking for receive transfers half as often (21 times against 44); 8 is 5% slower |
| the SoftMAC reorder window never moves back and never drops frames it gave up on | dropped frames filled xnet's pool and stopped every connection |

Recovery:

- When the host controller stops asking for receive transfers under heavy sending,
  they are taken back and queued again after about 130 ms. The card is not reset.
- After 4 receive errors in a row the ring is parked, and a receive watchdog resets
  the device. Before, resubmitting at once looped on the USB processor and hung the
  console.
- A transmit watchdog does the same for a stuck transmit.
- Every transmit completion calls `net_tx_done()`. Without it xnet's queue stalls
  under load, which looks like a console hang.

## Issues

- **Out of the console it stops at 167 to 170 Mbit/s (20.9 to 21.2 MB/s), at any
  number of streams;** in at 203 to 205 Mbit/s (25.4 to 25.6 MB/s) from 8 to 32
  streams. Both are near the console's USB: the RTL8156B stops at 175 out and 214
  to 223 in, and the RTL8822BU moves more bytes per frame (a 48-byte descriptor
  out, 24 plus PHY status in, the 802.11 and CCMP headers). Dropping the PHY
  status (32 bytes a frame) moved nothing measurable. Receiving takes about 26%
  of a thread (bench=prof), so it is not the processor.
- **2.4 GHz sent slowly (fixed 2026-11-03).** Out was a third of in (39 to 45
  against 132 Mbit/s), the AP taking the console at MCS 4 to 10 and hearing it 8 dB
  weaker than Linux with the dongle in the same place. Not the place, not the USB
  power (a bench supply on the dongle's 5 V changed nothing), not the TX power.
  Two causes, both rtw88 behaviour the port lacked: the antenna switch (0x4C bits
  23/24, rtw88's coex init; 46d1693: out 30 -> 85-96 Mbit/s, path A as loud as
  Linux) and the DIG (fb48462: the tables' IGI 0x20 took noise for frames, held
  sends and lost the AP's acknowledgements; 0x40-0x48 now). On 5 GHz neither
  changes anything measurable (IGI 0x20/0x30/0x40, switch on/off: within 2%).
  Path B is still heard 5 to 10 dB below path A on 2.4 GHz where Linux, same
  place, is 2 to 3 dB apart. Not the TX power words (Linux's give the same gap on
  the console), not the RF tables (identical to rtw88's usbmon), not the host-set
  BB/MAC state after the join (equal to Linux's); left: the firmware's calibration.
- **2.4 GHz scans missed the access point (fixed 2026-11-04, b454758).** With the
  AP a metre away no scan heard it while weaker networks were, and boots fell back
  to the onboard port. 0x4C's byte 2 read 0x62 from power-on (GPIO 13/14 the Wi-Fi's,
  LED2 in a hardware mode); rtw88's LED init leaves 0x20 and so does this now.
- **Sessions ended after an hour (fixed 2026-11-04, 30cffd7 and a86efd3).** The
  station did not answer the group key handshake, so the access point gave it up
  at its first group rekey (hostapd: hourly by default); four soaks ended at 55
  to 60 min, the router's `wpa_group_rekey` at 120 s ended one at 90 s. Every join
  after it then failed at the four-way: leaving had reported the AP's MAC ID
  disconnected to the firmware, which held the handshake's answers until a link
  that could not come. Both fixed; the group rekey is in wpa.c, so the AIC8800's
  station has it too.
- **8 streams into the console on 2.4 GHz can wedge xnet.** At 128 KB receive
  buffers 8 streams are 1 MB of windows on a pool of about 1 MB; on the lossier
  band connections stopped, never closed (the server still had 8 active with none
  left on the PC), and every later download stalled until a reboot. 4 streams held.
  (xnet has no SACK; see README, Configuration.)
- **About 10% below 2026-10-30 on 5 GHz,** ftpdll included (see Measured). Cause not
  known yet: the air after the router's reset, or a change in the driver.
- **The channel matters.** Channel 100 turned 28% busy with the console idle and
  cost 20 to 30%; hence channel 116.

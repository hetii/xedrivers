# rtl8156: Realtek RTL8156 / RTL8156B

Directory: `src/drivers/rtl8156/` · `0bda:8156` · USB 2.5G Ethernet

The `cdc` driver ([cdc.md](cdc.md)) plus the Realtek part: the 2.5G
advertisement, registers, the PHY and the chip version. The Realtek registers and PHY
window are in `src/drivers/realtek/`, shared with rtl8153 ([rtl8153.md](rtl8153.md)).

## How it works

1. The card is switched to its CDC-ECM configuration. Its own firmware then runs
   the MAC.
2. The PHY is told over Realtek vendor requests to advertise up to 2.5GBASE-T. In
   ECM the firmware alone offers only 10/100.
3. Frames go through the `cdc` transport: ECM by default, NCM if built or switched
   so.

Vendor configuration 1 (Realtek's own protocol) is not used. It would need the
whole of r8152's initialisation.

On a running console:

```sh
tools/xectl.py advertise 2500|1000|100|default
tools/xectl.py phy 0xa400 [value]          # PHY register (OCP address)
tools/xectl.py reg pla|usb 0xe610 [value]  # MAC register word
```

## Measured

The current figures are in [speedtest.jsonl](../speedtest.jsonl), with tables in the
README.

2026-11-03 (e500b72, with the NAK reload of src/core/usb.c), console and PC on a
1 Gbit/s router, xnet defaults, 8 streams for TCP:

| Transport | Test | Console -> PC | PC -> console |
|---|---|---|---|
| ECM | TCP RAM to RAM | 181 Mbit/s (22.7 MB/s) | 215 Mbit/s (26.9 MB/s) |
| NCM | TCP RAM to RAM | 190 Mbit/s (23.8 MB/s) | 224 Mbit/s (28.0 MB/s) |
| ECM | FTP 256 MB, Hdd1 | 166 Mbit/s (20.7 MB/s) | 162 Mbit/s (20.3 MB/s) |
| NCM | FTP 256 MB, Hdd1 | 168 Mbit/s (21.0 MB/s) | 145 Mbit/s (18.1 MB/s), 8 KB receive blocks (61f0824); 106 (13.3) with 16 KB |

The NAK reload took sending 175 -> 181-186 Mbit/s over ECM and 179-183 -> 190-193
over NCM; receiving did not move.

NCM is 4 to 5% faster over TCP; uploads over FTP were a third slower with 16 KB receive blocks and are 10% slower with 8 KB, so ECM stays
the default. 1, 4, 8 and 32 streams give the same totals.

Other servers on the console, 2026-10-19:

| Server | Console -> PC | PC -> console |
|---|---|---|
| XBDM getfile / sendfile | 169 Mbit/s (21.1 MB/s) | 188 Mbit/s (23.5 MB/s) |
| the onboard port (100M) with ftpdll, for comparison | 91 Mbit/s (11.4 MB/s) | 86 Mbit/s (10.7 MB/s) |

Two or four FTP sessions at once carried 155 and 154 Mbit/s (19.4 and 19.3 MB/s) in
total, no more than one: out of the console the path is the limit, not ftpdll's
session.

The driver alone, without xnet (`MODE=standalone`, UDP generator `xectl.py blast`,
2026-10-19):

| Direction | Rate |
|---|---|
| console -> PC | 188 Mbit/s (23.5 MB/s) |
| PC -> console | 242 Mbit/s (30.3 MB/s) |

Both directions at once with the xnet defaults: 106 Mbit/s (13.3 MB/s) each way,
where xnet's stock buffers gave 41 Mbit/s (5.1 MB/s) out and 148 Mbit/s
(18.5 MB/s) in. One direction alone did not move: the wire was at the card's
ceiling already.

Doubling the NCM block (16 to 32 KB, 8.7 to 12.6 frames each) gained nothing.

## Issues

- **Into the console it stops at about 27 MB/s (ECM) and 28 MB/s (NCM)**, at 1, 8 and
  32 streams alike. On a PC the card does 39 MB/s in, so the card can do more;
  whether the limit is the console's USB or how the card and the console's EHCI
  work together is not known yet.
- **Out of the console it stops at 21 to 24 MB/s**, like the RTL8822BU. The model
  fitted by sweeping the frame size: 10.9 us a transfer plus 36.9 ns a byte, so
  23.3 MB/s at the Ethernet MTU. That is why packing frames (NCM) moves nothing
  out.
- **FTP uploads stop at about 20.5 MB/s.** A capture shows the console advertising a
  zero window 23.8% of the time, in stalls of about 12 ms. ftpdll receives, then
  writes the disk, and receives nothing while it writes. 20.4 / (1 - 0.238) =
  26.8 MB/s, the RAM-to-RAM figure. Its thread's priority changes nothing. A server
  that receives while it writes would get the rest.
- **USB devices on one host controller share it.** With an AIC8800 on the same
  controller the card measured 146 to 156 Mbit/s (18.3 to 19.5 MB/s) out and 173 to
  187 Mbit/s (21.6 to 23.4 MB/s) in.
